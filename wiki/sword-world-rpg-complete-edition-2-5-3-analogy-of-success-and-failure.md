# 2.5.3 Analogy of Success and Failure

Source: [[sword-world-rpg-complete-edition]]

## Statements

- That is precisely the reason why we use difficulty checks. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- If a success roll is made for a difficulty check, the success or failure of the action is strictly unknown to the player. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- However, a player is given only one criteria to determine whether the success roll they made for a difficulty check is a success or a failure. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- That is : the dice that the players themselves have rolled. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- Therefore, if after examining the door, you do not find a trap, the risk is quite low even if you assume that there really are no traps. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- So, even if you examine the door and do not find any traps, you should be skeptical of the results. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- In particular, when a player rolls either of the two extremes, double sixes or double ones , it means automatic success or automatic failure , so that the player can clearly see whether the success roll is successful or not. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- A player's dice roll should be interpreted as an expression of confidence in the character's actions. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- A high roll indicates a high level of confidence, while a low roll indicates a lack of confidence in what they've done. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- It has been noted before that double sixes and double ones are automatic successes or failures and do not imply definitive successes or failures. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- For example, if a player is trying to find a trap on a door and has rolled double ones, the game master will of course tell the player you didn't find any traps . (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- When the player hears this, if they reply like the following, it's a prime example of this misinterpretation: Oh, I screwed up big time. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- It is especially bad to misinterpret an automatic failure as a definitive failure. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- Which means I can say for sure 'There are definitely no traps on this door'. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
- Consider it like I never checked the door. (Sword World RPG - Complete Edition.pdf p.32)
