# The Antikythera Mechanism

## The Antikythera Mechanism

- The Antikythera mechanism is an ancient Greek hand-powered orrery, often described as the oldest known analog computer. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00002))_
- It was retrieved from the sea in 1901 from a shipwreck off the coast of the Greek island of Antikythera, and is dated to roughly 150–100 BC. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00002))_
- By turning a hand crank, the user could advance a pointer across a dial showing the position of the Sun and Moon in the zodiac, the phase of the Moon, and the dates of solar and lunar eclipses predicted by the Saros cycle. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00003))_
- A separate dial tracked the four-year cycle of the Olympiad and other Panhellenic games. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00003))_
- The device consists of at least 30 meshing bronze gears housed in a wooden case roughly the size of a shoebox. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00003))_
- Its gear-cutting precision implies an established workshop tradition, yet no comparable device or workshop has been found. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00004))_
- The inscriptions suggest the device may have originated in Corinth or its colonies, possibly linking it to the school of Archimedes in Syracuse. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00005))_
- The Antikythera Mechanism Research Project, beginning in 2005, used microfocus X-ray computed tomography to read inscriptions hidden inside the corroded fragments — effectively a user's manual engraved on the device's plates. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00005))_
- Derek de Solla Price published the first detailed scientific study in 1974, using X-ray imaging to count gear teeth. _(antikythera-mechanism.md (source-range-90276921-00005))_
