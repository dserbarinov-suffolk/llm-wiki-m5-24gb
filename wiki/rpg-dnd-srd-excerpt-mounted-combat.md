# Mounted Combat

What [[rpg-dnd-srd-excerpt]] covers about mounted combat:

## Statements

### Mounted Combat

- A knight charging into battle on a warhorse, a wizard casting spells from the back of a griffon, or a cleric soaring through the sky on a pegasus all enjoy the benefits of speed and mobility that a mount can provide. A willing creature that is at least one size larger than you and that has _(rpg_dnd_srd_excerpt.pdf (source-range-4449ec9f-00466))_

- an appropriate anatomy can serve as a mount, using the following rules. _(rpg_dnd_srd_excerpt.pdf (source-range-4449ec9f-00467))_

### Mounted Combat / Controlling a Mount

- While you're mounted, you have two options. You can either control the mount or allow it to act independently. Intelligent creatures, such as dragons, act independently. You can control a mount only if it has been trained to accept a rider. Domesticated horses, donkeys, and similar creatures are assumed to have such training. The initiative of a controlled mount changes to match yours when you mount it. It moves as you direct it, and it has only three action options: Dash, Disengage, and Dodge. A controlled _(rpg_dnd_srd_excerpt.pdf (source-range-4449ec9f-00472))_

- mount can move and act even on the turn that you mount it. _(rpg_dnd_srd_excerpt.pdf (source-range-4449ec9f-00473))_

- An independent mount retains its place in the initiative order. Bearing a rider puts no restrictions on the actions the mount can take, and it moves and acts as it wishes. It might flee from combat, rush to attack and devour a badly injured foe, or otherwise act against your wishes. _(rpg_dnd_srd_excerpt.pdf (source-range-4449ec9f-00474))_
