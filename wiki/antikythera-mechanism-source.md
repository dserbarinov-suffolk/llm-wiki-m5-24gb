## Key supported claims

- The Antikythera mechanism is an ancient Greek mechanical device, often considered the earliest known analog computer. (raw/antikythera-mechanism.md)
- It enabled users to track celestial positions, lunar phases, and eclipse predictions using a hand crank. (raw/antikythera-mechanism.md)
- The device's inscriptions indicate a possible origin in Corinth or its colonies, potentially linking it to Archimedes' school in Syracuse. (raw/antikythera-mechanism.md)
- Its gear-cutting precision suggests a specialized workshop tradition, though no comparable devices or workshops have been found. (raw/antikythera-mechanism.md)
- Advanced imaging techniques revealed hidden inscriptions, providing insights into the device's functions and origins. (raw/antikythera-mechanism.md)
