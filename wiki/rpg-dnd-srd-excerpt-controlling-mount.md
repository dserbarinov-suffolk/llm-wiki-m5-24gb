# Controlling a Mount

What [[rpg-dnd-srd-excerpt]] covers about controlling a mount:

## Statements

### Controlling a Mount

- While you're mounted, you have two options. You can either control the mount or allow it to act independently. Intelligent creatures, such as dragons, act independently. You can control a mount only if it has been trained to accept a rider. Domesticated horses, donkeys, and similar creatures are assumed to have such training. The initiative of a controlled mount changes to match yours when you mount it. It moves as you direct it, and it has only three action options: Dash, Disengage, and Dodge. - mount can move and act even on the turn that you mount it. _(rpg_dnd_srd_excerpt.pdf (source-range-b7d08967-00464))_

- An independent mount retains its place in the initiative order. Bearing a rider puts no restrictions on the actions the mount can take, and it moves and acts as it wishes. It might flee from combat, rush to attack and devour a badly injured foe, or otherwise act against your wishes. _(rpg_dnd_srd_excerpt.pdf (source-range-b7d08967-00465))_
